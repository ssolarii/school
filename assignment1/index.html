Explain the pattern

1. Which district has the highest and lowest density?

Belize has the highest density, 27.0 people per km². Toledo has the lowest, 8.0 people per km².

2. Does the largest population mean the highest density?

In this dataset, Belize has both the largest population (113,630) and highest density (27.0). This is not a general rule: Cayo has 99,105 people and 18.6 people per km², while Corozal has only 45,310 people but 24.4 people per km². Land area changes the result.

3. How does Belize compare with the Caribbean?

Belize averages 17.3 people per km², compared with 256.2 in Jamaica and 625.8 in Barbados. Jamaica is about 14.8 times as dense as Belize; Barbados is about 36.2 times as dense. Barbados uses 2021; the other populations use 2022, so these are broad comparisons.

4. What could the pattern mean for services?

Belize and Cayo together contain 212,735 people, about 53.5% of the national population. This may increase demand for housing, school places and healthcare in parts of these districts. Low average density in Toledo may make travel to services harder for some residents. Population figures alone do not prove shortages or travel difficulties; facility locations, roads and local data would be needed.

5. Which technology could help?

Digital health appointments could help residents in remote Toledo communities arrange care and reduce unnecessary booking trips. Some users may lack reliable mobile coverage, electricity, devices or digital skills. A practical response is a staffed community access point, low-bandwidth SMS booking, telephone support and an in-person option. Remote appointments cannot replace care that needs a physical examination.

6. Answer to the guiding question

An interactive population map lets us select districts and compare where people live. Belize has 113,630 people and a density of 27.0 people per km², while Toledo has 37,124 people and 8.0 people per km². These differences can guide questions about where schools and health services may be needed, although district averages cannot show each village’s situation. Digital health bookings could improve access for some remote residents if community access points and SMS or telephone alternatives are provided. The map supports planning, but decisions also need local evidence about roads, facilities and connectivity.

Individual reflection

I learned that density compares population with land area, so a district with more people can still be less dense. The calculation was corrected to use total population divided by total land area for the national density, rather than an average of district densities. Definitions

Population is the number of people living in a place. Population density is the average number of people per square kilometre. A census is an official population count. A demographic group shares a characteristic such as age. A choropleth map shades areas according to a data measure.
